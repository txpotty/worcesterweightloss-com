    What Compounded Semaglutide Is in Framingham

    Compounded semaglutide is prepared by a licensed compounding pharmacy for one patient based on a provider's prescription. It uses the same active ingredient as Wegovy and Ozempic. It is not FDA-approved. That means the FDA has not reviewed compounded semaglutide for safety or effectiveness, and it does not inspect each batch the way it oversees brand-name manufacturing. Federal rules limit compounding copies of approved drugs, so a provider must document a clinical reason.

    Who Compounded Semaglutide Is For

    Candidates are adults with a BMI of 30 or higher, or 27 or higher with a weight-related condition. It can make sense for a patient who needs a smaller step between standard doses. Some patients prefer brand-name. Wegovy remains the FDA-approved option, and that matters to many people. The provider weighs your coverage, your past side effects and any documented need for a personalized dose before recommending one path or the other.

    Dosing and Schedule in Framingham

    Injections happen once a week under the skin of the belly or thigh, drawn from a vial with an insulin syringe. Treatment usually starts around 0.25 mg weekly. Units are not milligrams.     The first month at a low dose is mostly about tolerance. Nausea may show up after the first doses and again after each increase. Portions shrink by month two. Clinical trial results such as STEP 1 were based on brand-name semaglutide, so outcomes with compounded versions have not been studied the same way and your provider tracks your own progress closely.

    Side Effects of Compounded Semaglutide in Framingham

    Nausea, diarrhea, constipation and vomiting are the most common side effects, mostly after starting or raising a dose. Dosing mistakes can cause severe versions of these symptoms. Check every syringe twice. Rarer but serious risks include pancreatitis, gallbladder disease, kidney injury from dehydration and low blood sugar when combined with insulin or a sulfonylurea. A boxed warning covers thyroid C-cell tumors found in rodent studies.

    The intake asks about a personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer or MEN2, which rules semaglutide out. Your provider also checks for past pancreatitis, gallbladder disease, diabetic retinopathy, severe stomach emptying problems and any history of an eating disorder before writing a prescription. Pregnancy rules it out. Expect a question about your comfort drawing medication from a vial.

    Wegovy is FDA-approved, comes in prefilled pens with fixed doses and has years of trial data. Compounded versions need manual measuring and lack that approval. Insurance treats them differently.
