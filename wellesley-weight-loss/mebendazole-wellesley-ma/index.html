    How Mebendazole Treatment Works in Wellesley, MA

    How Mebendazole Works in Wellesley

    Mebendazole blocks a worm's ability to take in glucose by disrupting tiny structures inside its cells called microtubules. The worms starve and die. Dead worms leave in the stool over the next few days, often without being seen, and that is completely expected. Only a small amount is absorbed into the blood. In the US, it is sold as an FDA-approved chewable tablet.

    Who Mebendazole Is For

    Adults usually ask for it because of pinworm. The giveaway is anal itching at night, often in a household where school-age children brought it home from a classroom or daycare. It also treats roundworm, whipworm and hookworm, which are more often linked to travel or certain environments. Adults with a positive test qualify. Weight loss and cleanses are not on the list.

    Mebendazole Dosing and Schedule in Wellesley

    For pinworm, the usual plan is a single 100 mg chewable tablet, repeated in two weeks. The repeat dose targets worms that hatched after the first one, since eggs survive treatment. Other intestinal worms are often treated with 100 mg twice daily for three days, followed by a stool test in some cases. Chew it fully. Meals do not matter.

    What to Expect After Taking Mebendazole

    Most people feel nothing after the dose. A few notice mild cramping or loose stools for a day. Pinworm itching often improves within three to five days, though some irritation can linger for a week or more while the skin around the anus settles. You may not see worms. The repeat dose at week two is the step people forget, so set a reminder.

    Side Effects of Mebendazole in Wellesley

    Because so little is absorbed, mebendazole is well tolerated at short courses. Possible side effects include abdominal pain, diarrhea, gas and nausea. Rash is rare. Longer, high-dose courses used for other conditions carry added risks, like lower white blood cell counts or liver effects, which is why providers stick to standard dosing here. Message your provider about a rash, fever or severe stomach pain.

    Eligibility and Screening

    Your intake asks about symptoms, timing, household members, recent travel and any test results. The provider will ask about pregnancy. Mebendazole is generally avoided in the first trimester, and the provider also screens for breastfeeding, liver disease and interacting drugs. Metronidazole is the big interaction, since combining the two has been linked to rare severe skin reactions. Non-pinworm infections usually need a stool test first.

    How Mebendazole Compares to Other Options in Wellesley

    Albendazole works the same way and is sometimes used for the same infections. Pyrantel pamoate, sold without a prescription, also treats pinworm by paralyzing the worms. Ivermectin is preferred for strongyloides, scabies and lice, rather than pinworm. When the type of parasite is still unclear, the antiparasitic protocol starts with that question and may recommend testing. Your provider recommends the drug with the best evidence.
