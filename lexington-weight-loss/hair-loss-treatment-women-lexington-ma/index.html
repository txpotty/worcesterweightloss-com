    How Women's Hair Loss Treatment Works in Lexington, MA

    How Women's Hair Loss Treatment Works in Lexington

    Female pattern hair loss widens the part and thins the top while the front hairline usually stays put. Inherited sensitivity to androgens gradually shrinks follicles on the crown and part. Minoxidil lengthens the growth phase and thickens strands. Off-label spironolactone dampens androgen effects on hair.

    Who Women's Treatment Is For

    It suits adult women with gradual thinning, a widening part or less volume on top. Postpartum and post-illness shedding is worth a review too, even if it often resolves. Patchy loss is different. Bald spots or a painful scalp call for a dermatologist visit. Your photos help sort it out.

    Dosing and Schedule in Lexington

    Topical minoxidil is FDA-approved for women as a 5 percent foam once a day or a 2 percent solution twice a day. Some women prefer off-label oral minoxidil at a low daily dose instead of a topical. Spironolactone starts low, often 25 to 50 mg, and is raised slowly. Topical blends are applied daily.

    What to Expect Month by Month

    Expect some extra shedding in months one and two. It is usually temporary, and it happens because minoxidil pushes resting hairs out so new growing hairs can replace them, which feels backward but is a normal sign. By months three to six, shedding slows and new growth may appear along the part. Full results take about a year.

    Side Effects of Women's Treatment in Lexington

    Scalp itch, dryness and fine facial hair, if the liquid drips onto the forehead or cheeks, are the usual topical minoxidil complaints. Oral minoxidil can cause extra body or facial hair, ankle swelling or a faster heartbeat, which is why it starts at a low dose. Spotting and breast soreness are common with spironolactone. It can also raise potassium. Report palpitations promptly.

    Eligibility and Screening

    Pregnancy, breastfeeding and plans to conceive come first in the review. Spironolactone and DHT blockers can affect a developing fetus, so your provider confirms reliable contraception before prescribing either, and oral minoxidil is avoided during pregnancy too. Blood pressure and kidney health are reviewed. Iron and thyroid labs are common requests.

    How Women's Options Compare in Lexington

    Minoxidil is the base of most plans and the main FDA-approved option for women. Women with signs of excess androgens, like adult acne, often add off-label spironolactone. The minoxidil and GHK-Cu blend is compounded and lacks FDA approval. DHT blockers are usually reserved for postmenopausal women.
